**A:** Heat affects climbing training in a few concrete ways. Here's what to adjust:

## What changes with 30°C+

**Skin and finger tendons:** Heat softens skin faster and makes tendons slightly more pliable — which sounds good but means the tissue fatigues earlier and tears more easily under sustained load. Hangboard sessions need a shorter warm-up time (tendons reach working temp quicker) but also a lower total volume ceiling.

**Cardiovascular load:** The heart is doing double duty — cooling + training. RPE will run 1-2 points higher than normal for the same absolute effort. Trust the RPE, not the prescribed intensity.

**Hydration:** Even mild dehydration (1-2% body weight) measurably reduces grip strength and concentration. Sweat rate climbing outdoors at 30°C can hit 1-1.5 L/hour.

---

## Practical adjustments for your situation

**Outdoor at Berdorf** (you've been going regularly):
- Climb in the morning or evening — shade timing matters more than almost anything else
- Add 10 min to your rest between attempts (heat slows lactate clearance)
- Bring 1.5–2 L water minimum, drink before you're thirsty
- If skin starts shredding early, stop — it's the heat, not weakness, and torn skin costs you days

**Home hangboard sessions:**
- Do these at the coolest part of the day (early morning ideal)
- Drop total hang volume ~15-20% if the room is above 28°C — the fatigue accumulates faster than it feels
- Keep a fan directly on your forearms during rests; it genuinely helps recovery between sets

**This week specifically:** you've already had a solid week (8a outdoor on Friday, good finger session Thursday). Today is rest. That's exactly right — don't add anything.
