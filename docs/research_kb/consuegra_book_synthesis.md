## Chapter 7 — Analysis of the Main Physiological Factors in Climbing Performance

### 7.1 The Old Paradigm (Debunked)

Consuegra explicitly rejects the athletics-based model of climbing physiology:
- Comparing climbing to running (4-move boulder = 100m sprint, etc.) is an "epic fail"
- Climbing involves intermittent isometric contractions of SMALL muscles (forearms), not cyclical contractions of LARGE muscle groups
- The whole-body systemic response (heart, lungs, liver glycogen) that powers cycling/running does NOT apply to climbing
- **Lactate levels in climbing are only 5-7 mmol/L** (Gáspari 2015, La Torre 2009, Billat 1995, Schöffl 2006) vs 17 mmol/L in 400m sprint and 29 mmol/L in BMX Wingate tests
- This proves anaerobic glycolysis is NOT the dominant energy system in climbing

### 7.2 General Performance Factors (Whole-Body Level)

Three main factors at the general level:

**1. Physical factors (how we move on the wall):**
- Strength: pulling, stabilization, propulsion
- Mobility: determines positions accessible on the wall

**2. Strength-to-weight ratio:**
- Elite climbers have very low body fat % (Couceiro PhD 2010)
- Fat is not contractile — it's "dead weight"
- What matters is "useful weight" (contractile tissue)
- MVC/N (force relative to body weight in newtons) is significantly higher in elite vs. advanced vs. intermediate vs. non-climbers (Fryer 2015b, 2015c)
- Example: 67kg climber with +45kg pull-up = 1.67 ratio; 80kg climber needing 10kg assist = 0.875 ratio

**3. Injuries:**
- Without well-designed plan → exponential injury risk
- Covers: soft tissue protection, postural correction, agonist/antagonist balance, optimal loading

### 7.3 Local Performance Factors (Forearm — The Bottleneck)

**A. Strength (two sub-components):**

1. **Maximum grip strength** — key deciding factor (Schöffl 2006, Magiera 2013, Ozimek 2016, López-Rivera 2009)
   - Inability to produce/maintain force on holds = main reason for falling (Vigouroux & Quaine 2006)
   - Boulderers have much higher MVC than sport climbers (Fryer 2017)
   - Elite (8b/8c) hang with 14% more weight than advanced (7c+/8a) on 3s max test (Ozimek 2017)

2. **RFD (Rate of Force Development)** — deciding factor for boulderers
   - Boulderers' RFD is 36.73% higher than sport climbers (Fanchini 2013)
   - RFD200ms distinguishes climbing ability levels (Levernier & Laffaye 2019)
   - Critical for contact strength: hitting a sloper requires generating force in <200ms

**B. Endurance (the main differentiator for route climbing):**

Key metric: **Force-Time Integral (FTI)** = 0.4 × contraction duration (s) × force (N)
- Directly proportional to climbing level (Fryer 2015b)
- Measured via dynamometer test to exhaustion

Endurance is determined by three things:

1. **FTI capacity** — how much force over how long
2. **Anaerobic alactic metabolism** — SURPRISE: the dominant ATP pathway for forearms (Bertuzzi 2007), NOT glycolysis
   - Isometric contractions in crux sections last 10-12s max (López-Rivera 2014d)
   - Aerobic system replenishes PCr during brief rest between moves
3. **Muscle oxidative capacity** — "the true crux of the matter"
   - FDP oxidative capacity is directly proportional to max redpoint grade (Saul 2019, 350+ studies review)
   - Dependent on:
     - **Forearm blood flow**: elite climbers have wider arteries, better vasodilation, less metaboreflex pressure on arteries, capillary angiogenesis (MacLeod 2007, Philippe 2012, Fryer 2015a)
     - **Elite climbers reoxygenate 83% faster** than intermediate (Fryer 2015a)
     - **Deoxygenation/reoxygenation capacity**: the BIGGEST determining factor of forearm endurance (Fryer 2016). Measurable via NIRS.

**C. Efficiency — "economy of movement is more important than a good metabolism" (Bertuzzi 2007)**
- Elite climbers use up to **1/5 (20%) of the energy** of novices on the same circuit (Baláš 2014b)
- More weight through feet = **11% less energy, 14% lower heart rate** (Baláš 2014a)
- Elite climbers produce more ATP via alactic + aerobic pathways, avoiding glycolytic pathway → lower lactate (Saul 2019)
- Efficiency comes from: technique, visualization, footwork, motor skills, psychology, experience

## Chapter 10 — Periodisation Models: In Search of Optimal Peak Form

### 10.1 Basic Concepts

- **Macrocycle**: extended period resulting in higher performance (not necessarily = 1 year)
- **Mesocycle**: medium-length block for intermediate goals
- **Microcycle**: short block for individual skills/qualities
- "Defining these as long, medium and short blocks avoids labelling macrocycle as a year"
- Multiple macrocycles possible per season

Consuegra covers: 2 linear models (traditional + reverse) and 1 block model (ATR)

### 10.2 Tapering — Mujika & Bosquet (2016) Guidelines

**Average performance improvement from quality tapering: 1.96%** (seems small but at elite level = difference between 1st and 4th place)

**Mujika & Bosquet tapering rules:**
1. Reduce training volume 60-41% over 4 weeks before competition
2. First reduce number of sessions, then reduce duration
3. Intensity should remain CONSTANT (not increase)
4. Last few sessions should NOT be used as a test
5. Adapt calorie intake to reduced energy needs (avoid weight gain)
6. Prioritize movement efficiency and maximum power development
7. Favour heavy weights over light weights
8. Ensure full recovery
9. When done correctly: increases fast-twitch fiber diameter, boosts neural electrical activity, improves glycolysis efficiency, modifies anabolic hormonal response

**Key quote (via Mujika):** "You don't need to feel good to swim fast" — feelings during taper may be negative but body is supercompensating

### 10.3 Linear Periodisation (Matveyev 1977)

**Structure:** General prep → Specific prep → Competition → Transition

**Base/Preparation period:**
- *General preparation (20-30% for advanced, 60-70% for less advanced):*
  - Multilateral, non-specific, volume > intensity
  - Build movement variety, basic technique, climbing mileage
  - Continuity and endurance focus
- *Specific preparation:*
  - Intensity increases, volume decreases
  - Max strength, power, RFD
  - Specific routes/boulder types matching goal

**Competition period:**
- Significant volume drop, maintain intensity
- Focus on specific wall-based climbing at goal-relevant difficulty
- Includes tapering protocol

**Transition period:** Recovery and regeneration

**Advantages:** Strong foundations, low injury risk, safe for beginners
**Disadvantages:** Long time to peak, no readjustment, not for advanced athletes, fallen into disuse

### 10.4 Example: Linear Periodisation for Novice (F5s → F6s)

**Macrocycle: September → April (8 months)**

| Mesocycle | Duration | Physical Conditioning | Climbing | Endurance |
|-----------|----------|----------------------|----------|-----------|
| 1 (General) | 4 weeks | Circuits all muscle groups, EL 8(20) | Easy traverses, build moves | Z1 aerobic 2h/week |
| 2 (General) | 6 weeks | Hypertrophy, EL 8(16), core/shoulders | Technique: bicycles, foot swaps | Intermittent method 12min |
| 3 (General) | 6 weeks | Hypertrophy, EL 6(12) | Overhangs, heel/toe hooks | Long interval 20min |
| 4 (Specific) | 6 weeks | Max strength multi-joint | Active wall-based recovery, max grip 1:30 pull-up bar | Continuous method |
| 5 (Specific) | 5 weeks | Max strength multi-joint | HIIT short intervals, max grip 1:30 on 3-pad hold | High-intensity intervals |
| Taper | 3 weeks | None | Short intervals overhanging + max pulling + max grip | Endurance training |
| Peak form | 1 week | — | Sport climbing trip | — |

### 10.5 Reverse Linear Periodisation (Ian King 2000)

**Key difference:** Start with maximum intensity/minimum volume, gradually reduce intensity and increase volume (opposite of Matveyev).

**Rationale (House & Johnston 2014):** Best for climbers and mountaineers because:
- Recruits fast-twitch fibers first (high intensity)
- Then converts them to slow-twitch (endurance) through training
- Results in higher fibril recruitment and enhanced mitochondrial density

**Advantages:** Strong foundations, best for trainees/beginners, higher Type II recruitment
**Disadvantages:** Same as traditional LP — long to peak, no readjustment, not for advanced

### 10.6 ATR Periodisation (Issurin & Kaverin 1985)

**THE KEY MODEL — used by Patxi Usobiaga (who coached Adam Ondra)**

**Structure:** Short concentrated blocks, 6-9 weeks per macrocycle → multiple peaks per season

**Three mesocycles:**

| Mesocycle | Duration | Goal | Volume | Intensity | Content (Climbing) |
|-----------|----------|------|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Accumulation** | 2-3 weeks | Build technical + movement potential | HIGH | Moderate | Basic endurance, strength, technique, correction of errors, varied bouldering/routes, ROM, pulling strength |
| **Transmutation** | 2-3 weeks | Transform potential into sport-specific ability | Lower | HIGH | Specific strength, finger endurance, power, RFD, technique under fatigue. Must be well-rested. |
| **Realisation** | 2-3 weeks | Achieve peak form for competition/project | Low | MAX | Competition-specific climbing, consolidate target grade, tapering + recovery protocols |

**Microcycle types within each mesocycle:**

| Type | Description | Duration | Load |
|------|-----------|----------|------|
| **Adjustment** | Medium load, medium intensity, medium difficulty | 4-7 days | Medium |
| **Load** | Significant load and total workload | 1 week | High |
| **Impact** | Maximum workload, extreme limits | 1 week | Very High |
| **Activation** | Low volume, increased intensity, competition modeling | 3-7 days | Low vol / High int |
| **Competition** | Competition schedule + recovery protocols | 3-9 days | Variable |
| **Recovery** | Low volume/intensity, varied recovery methods | 3-7 days | Low |

**Microcycle distribution across mesocycles:**

| | Accumulation | Transmutation | Realisation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Option 1 | Recovery/Adjustment | Adjustment + Load | Adjustment or Load |
| Option 2 | Load + Impact | Load + Impact | Load or Recovery |
| Option 3 | Load | Load + Impact | Activation + Competition |

**Advantages:** Multiple peak points, quick peak form (6-9 weeks), best for high-level with competitions, varied = motivating
**Disadvantages:** Complex/time-consuming, doesn't allow consolidation/stabilization of skills

### 10.7 Example: ATR for Advanced Climber (Target F7c Route)

**Macrocycle: March → April (only ~7 weeks!)**

**Accumulation (4-21 March, 2.5 weeks):**
- Adjustment micro 1 (4 days): max strength multi-joint EL 1(4), technique, bouldering
- Load micro 1 (1 week): max strength EL 2(3), bouldering, max weight deadhangs large edge
- Load micro 2 (1 week): max strength EL 2(3), bouldering, power/RFD campus board

**Transmutation (22 March - 11 April, 3 weeks):**
- Load micro 3 (1 week): endurance continuous method + BFR 20min, then long interval 50min total
- Impact micro 1 (1 week): intermittent deadhangs, short intervals F7a/7a+ overhanging, active wall-based recovery 2min
- Impact micro 2 (1 week): intermittent deadhangs, medium intervals F7a/7a+ overhanging, wall-based recovery 1min 30s

**Realisation (12-21 April, ~10 days):**
- Adjustment micro 2 (4 days): bouldering, max weight deadhangs large edge
- Activation micro (3 days): bouldering, max pulling EL 1(3) + max grip minimum edge
- Competition micro (3 days): work on target route
