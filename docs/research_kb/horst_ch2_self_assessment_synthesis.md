## 1. Core Philosophy

Hörst's opening premise: improvement begins with **"Know thyself."** You cannot progress beyond your current state using the same thoughts and actions that brought you there. The primary directive of training is to address weaknesses, not to reinforce existing strengths — a trap most climbers fall into.

Key claims:
- The average climber wastes **50% or more** of their strength and energy due to flawed technique, inefficient movement, and poor mental control
- Technical and mental factors together constitute **two-thirds** of the performance puzzle
- Physical strength (the remaining third) is commonly over-emphasized and over-trained
- **Critical benchmark:** "If you can do ten fingertip pull-ups, you are probably strong enough to climb most 5.12a routes" — meaning strength is rarely the true limiter at the 5.12 / 7b level
- Underlying causes of failure are often subtle and not obvious from surface-level observation; they require deliberate introspection + external feedback

This framing strongly supports our existing KB evidence (Magiera 2013: technique + mental = ~55% of variance; Topic 01 §5) with a practitioner-accessible benchmark.

## 2. Methods of Objective Self-Evaluation

### 2.1 Internal Self-Assessment
Diagnostic questions for physical/technical weaknesses:
- Does my footwork deteriorate on hard sequences?
- Do I overgrip and hang on too long in hard moves?
- Do I climb too slowly through crux moves?
- Do I lack flexibility to reach key holds or find better body positions?
- Am I really too short for this move, or have I failed to find the body position that makes it possible?

Diagnostic questions for mental errors:
- Do I fail to see the sequence or do I try something new when the obvious doesn't work?
- Do I try too hard or give up too easily?
- Am I controlling my internal self-talk, or is the critic within doing a hatchet job on me?
- Do I monitor and control my body tension, or does my perceived pressure of the situation run the show?
- Do I sabotage myself before leaving the ground by doubting my ability and pondering past failures?

### 2.2 External Assessment
- **Video analysis**: most powerful tool — reveals technique flaws not perceptible in the moment
- **Coach feedback**: provides even more objective view of economy of effort and tactical patterns
- **Observable diagnostic signals**:
  - Feet skidding or popping off footholds → lack of attention to footwork
  - Constantly stretching for holds just out of reach → missing critical intermediate holds or using less effective body positions
  - Tight, mechanical, hesitant movement → indicates mental/tension problems

## 3. The Self-Assessment Test (30 Items)

### 3.1 Scoring Scale
| Score | Frequency |
|-------|-----------|
| 0 | Almost always (problem occurs almost every climb) |
| 1 | Often |
| 2 | About half the time |
| 3 | Occasionally |
| 4 | Seldom |
| 5 | Never (no problem) |

**Lower scores = more severe weakness.** Stars are placed on questions scoring 3 or less.

*(Note: Q1 appears on boundary Technical/Physical — Hörst places it in Physical. Q5: bicep pump before forearms is a technical signal, placed in Technique column.)*

### 3.4 Interpreting Results
- **All three domains within 5 points of each other** → balanced climber
- **One domain significantly lower** → that is the major weakness and primary training target
- Flag individual questions scoring ≤ 3 → these are specific skill gaps to address
- Appendix C (Hörst) provides additional coaching tips and training strategies for each question
- **Reassessment recommended**: annually, or when training feels flat / performance plateaus

## 4. The Cycle of Improvement

Hörst's process model for continuous development:

```
Self-Assessment → Set Goals → Take Action → Make Course Corrections → (loop)
```

This creates a **Spiral of Improvement** when all three stages are executed deliberately.

**Signs to stop and reassess** (before completing a full cycle):
- Plateau in performance
- Training feels flat or uninspiring
- Drop in motivation

**Protocol on these signs:** Take 1–2 weeks off, then retake the assessment and start a new cycle.

**Key distinction:** Year-round climbing without this deliberate cycle = "unfocused" → slow results and frequent plateaus.

## 5. Goal-Setting Framework

### 5.1 Three Time Frames

| Time Frame | Horizon | Purpose | Tools |
|-----------|---------|---------|-------|
| **Short-term** | Daily | Daily game plan — specific exercises, sets, reps, focus intentions | Written before each session |
| **Medium-term** | Weekly / Monthly | Shape and direction — climbing trips, big events, rest day maintenance | Calendar |
| **Long-term** | Yearly / Career | Mega goals — ultimate grade aspirations, dream routes | Written in black and white |

### 5.2 Goal-Setting Principles
1. Write goals down in **precise terms** with **realistic deadlines** — unwritten goals remain intangible hopes
2. Create a **mental picture** (visualization) of each goal
3. Write a **mission statement** summarizing the purpose of each time frame
4. Explicitly list **what you will give up** to achieve each goal — critical step most systems omit; you cannot add something without sacrificing something else
5. Share goals with a climbing partner or trusted person for **accountability**

### 5.3 Example Short-Term Goal Session Plan (from Hörst)
**Today's Mission:** Focus on improving footwork and conserving energy by practicing skills and techniques

*What I will give up:* Bouldering games, routes above on-sight ability, rushing home to watch TV

*What I will do:*
1. Warm up — 15 min light bouldering + gentle stretching + 2 sets pull-ups
2. 10–15 min traverse drill: light grip, precise footwork, smooth movement
3. Toprope several climbs within one number grade of limit — focus on **technique, not performance outcome** (fall or not fall irrelevant)
4. 3 × 6 reps One-Arm Lunging exercise
5. 3 × weighted pull-ups with 10 lb
6. 10 min wrist, rotator cuff, and scapular stabilizer training
7. 5–10 min stretching cool-down
8. Eat a meal within 1–2 hours post-workout
9. Get 7–8 hours sleep

### 5.4 Tips for Achieving Goals (Hörst's 6-Point Summary)
1. Know yourself. Live your passion. Identify what worthy goals will drive you to excellence
2. Regularly assess strengths and weaknesses — what is holding you back in terms of action or inaction?
3. Take the self-assessment at least once per year; consider getting objective evaluation from a coach
4. Regularly evaluate the effectiveness of your actions — are you obtaining the intended results? If not, make course corrections
5. Don't be afraid to step away from the crowd and pursue your own mega goals; ally with like-minded individuals
6. Set mega goals that will inspire and energize you from sunrise to sunset

- Hörst's "Mental" maps to our mental axis
- **No nutrition/recovery axis in Hörst's test** (he covers nutrition in Ch.11 separately)
