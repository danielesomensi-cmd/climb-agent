## Quick reference

Finger flexor strength is the single strongest predictor of climbing performance (r = 0.42-0.92 across studies). Three adaptation systems work on different timelines: **neural** (2-8 weeks, fast), **muscular** (4-8 months), **tendon/pulley** (1-3+ years). The engine trains all three through a layered protocol set: max hangs and overcoming isometrics for recruitment, intermittent dead hangs (repeaters) for endurance + hypertrophy, and density hangs for tendon work. Two methods cannot be combined in the same mesocycle (D49). All hangboard work is open-hand or half-crimp — never full crimp (D72, L0). Beginners with <2 years systematic training are blocked from advanced protocols (D35, L0); climbing volume is their finger training.

## Core findings

### 1. Physiology — what's actually adapting

Finger flexors (FDP, FDS) are small forearm muscles working through a long tendon and pulley system in the fingers. Climbing contraction:rest ratios are unfavorable (6.3s:1.5s in lead competition, 7.9s:0.6s in bouldering — Draga 2024). They work isometrically at high % MVC.

Three adaptation systems with very different timescales (Nelson, López, general S&C):

| Level | Adaptation | Timeline | Stimulus | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Neural** | Motor unit recruitment, firing rate | 2-8 weeks | High intensity (>85% MVC), short duration (3-7s), overcoming isometrics | CNS — high-threshold motor unit recruitment |
| **Muscular** | Hypertrophy of FDP, FDS, increased CSA | 4-8 months | 60-80% MVC, higher volume, yielding isometrics | Mechanical tension + metabolic stress |
| **Structural/tendon** | Collagen density, pulley strengthening | 1-3+ years | Long-duration isometrics (30-45s), 40-75% MVC | Fascicle sliding, collagen synthesis (Baar) |

**Nelson's pivotal framing:** "Muscles adapt in 4-8 months; connective tissues take years." Beginners can produce muscle gains fast — tendon capacity lags. This is *the* reason behind D35's hangboard experience gate.

### 2. Methods catalog — what the engine prescribes

#### Eva López — the three protocols

| Method | Intensity | Duration | Rest | Sets | Primary effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **MaxHangs MAW** (Max Added Weight) | 85-100% MVC | 5-10 s | 180-240 s | 3-5 | Neural recruitment, max strength |
| **MaxHangs MED** (Min Edge Depth) | 85-100% MVC, BW only | 10-12 s | 180-240 s | 3-5 | Max strength + grip specificity |
| **IntHangs** (intermittent dead hangs / repeaters) | 60-80% MVC | 7-15 s on / 3-15 s off | 60-180 s | 3-8 | Strength-endurance + hypertrophy |
| **SubHangs** (submaximal long hangs) | 55-85% MVC | 20-45 s | 30-120 s | 4-8 | Endurance, aerobic capacity |

Key López findings:
- López-Rivera & González-Badillo 2012: 2×4-week cycles of MAW vs MED in 7c+/8a climbers — both improved max finger strength; MAW slightly larger effect on max, MED more climbing-specific.
- López-Rivera & González-Badillo 2018: 8-week MaxHangs vs IntHangs vs Combination — IntHangs improved grip endurance after 4 weeks (p=0.004); MaxHangs improved grip endurance after 8 weeks (p=0.010). Combination = no synergy.
- López load-monitoring rule (D14): if edge drops >2 mm or weight drops >25% session-over-session → excessive fatigue, defer 1-2 days.

#### Tyler Nelson — recruitment + density + RFD

Three components (Nelson / Camp4 Human Performance):

| Component | Method | Intensity | Duration | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Recruitment** | Overcoming isometrics (pulling against immovable bar/edge) | RPE 10, max effort | 3-5 s | Recruit high-threshold motor units |
| **Density** | Yielding isometrics, hang to near-failure | 40-75% MVC | 30-45 s | Tendon density (collagen synthesis) |
| **Stiffness / RFD** | Short explosive pulls | Max RFD | 1-3 s | Contact strength |

Canonical Nelson density hangs: 20-40 s per rep at ~75% MVC (RPE 9-9.5), 2-3 reps per set, 2-3 sets per position, 3-5 min rest between sets, ~8 sets total. Open-hand preferred for tendon health.

Nelson on beginners (V0-V4): fingerboard as warm-up only, 5s on / 5s off at RPE 5-7, 40-35 mm edge. Climbing volume itself is the finger training.

#### Hörst protocols

- **7-53 Protocol** (signature): 7 s hang / 53 s rest × 3 reps × 3-5 sets. 53 s rest aligns with PCr resynthesis phase 1. Near-maximal intensity.
- **Maximum Weight 10s**: 10 s hang at ~90% MVC, 3 min rest, 5 hangs/set, 5 min between sets.
- **Minimum Edge**: 12 s hang on smallest holdable edge at BW, 3 min rest.
- **7/3 Repeaters**: 7s on / 3s off × 6 reps per set, 3 min between sets, 60-80% MVC.

Hörst order rule: hangboard *before* climbing (tendons fresh), no pump during strength protocols, max 5 sets per session.

#### Lattice — data-driven

- Test: 7 s max hang on 20 mm Lattice Rung, half crimp (D85). Compare to n>1000 dataset.
- Periodize: max hangs in Strength phase, repeaters in Endurance/Base phase.
- Note (2023+): Lattice has moved away from to-failure long hangs for strength purposes.

### 3. Recent practice shifts — lifting edge and Abrahangs

#### Lifting edge / no-hangs (Lattice 2024-2025 — major shift)

Lattice published (Hutchens, blog 2024-06-26) that *over 30% of their coaching plans now use lifting-edge workouts in place of fingerboard*. MXEdge Lift released May 2024 as a portable training tool. The lifting edge is a static edge or block lifted from the ground while the climber pulls upward through a sling and weight pin — same isometric stimulus, different ergonomics.

When to recommend lifting edge over hangboard:
- **Travel and minimum-kit weeks** — portable, low joint stress (see `18_equipment_fallback.md`).
- **Injury return** — finer load gradation (start very low, add 1-2 kg increments).
- **Supplementary frequency** — e.g., Abrahangs-style low-load 2×/day where hangboard would be too stressful.
- **Hangboard-resistant users** — wrist pain on overhang holds, shoulder issues with full-hang body position.

Hangboard is still appropriate for classic max-strength protocols, standardized testing (Lattice test is hangboard), and users with established hangboard adaptation.

**Evidence status:** Lattice and CAMP4 both moved toward lifting edge in coaching practice (2024-2025). No published head-to-head RCT against hangboard. The shift is practice-led, not evidence-led — coach should present both as valid options.

#### Abrahangs — Gilmore 2024

**Gilmore et al. 2024** (*Sports Medicine - Open* 10:125): retrospective analysis of n>500 climbers via the Crimpd app. Low-load, high-frequency hangs ("Abrahangs": ~10 min total, 2×/day, ~40% MVC) produced strength gains comparable to Max Hangs. Combining Abrahangs + Max Hangs → additive +5.8% over either alone. Mechanism: Baar 2017 (10-min loading saturates collagen synthesis pathway; needs ~6 hr to reset, so 2×/day is feasible).

**Critical limitations (CAMP4 Apr 2025 critique):**
- Retrospective design, self-selected load.
- Many users mixed Abrahangs with other protocols — can't fully isolate.
- "Suggestive, not conclusive" framing is appropriate.

**Engine v1 stance:** Abrahangs are an opt-in protocol gated to users with ≥6 months systematic hangboard experience (parallel to D35's framework). Coach explains the trade-off: high commitment (2×/day for the duration of a cycle), but adds a tendon stimulus without displacing Max Hangs.

### 4. Grip types

| Grip | Joint position | Pulley load | Force | Risk | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Half crimp** | PIP ~90°, DIP straight | Moderate | High | Moderate | **Default test grip, most versatile** |
| **Open hand / drag** | PIP extended, DIP slightly flexed | Low-moderate | Lower | Lowest | Slopers, base training, recovery |
| **Full crimp** | PIP ~90°, DIP hyperextended, thumb wrap | **Very high (A2)** | Highest | **Highest** | Wall climbing only — **never on hangboard (L0 D72)** |

Söderqvist 2024: half crimp (strong hand) alone explains 57% of bouldering variance; half crimp + front-3 drag = 66%. Half crimp is the most important grip to both train and test.

Engine training: default open hand for tendon-conditioning blocks (beginners + first 4 wk of any new protocol), transition to half crimp for max protocols, never prescribe full crimp on the hangboard (D72 / L0).

### 5. Training intervention evidence

Selected RCTs and controlled studies that anchor the engine's prescriptions:

- **Hermans 2022** — 10 weeks hangboard training (2×48 min/wk) vs control in 35 intermediate-advanced climbers. HBT increased peak and average force on 23 mm rung, increased utilization rate. **Verdict: 10 weeks structured hangboard at intermediate level produces measurable gains.**
- **Saeterbakken 2024 (RCT)** — 5 weeks dynamic finger flexor training in 31 advanced-elite boulderers. Increased 1RM dynamic finger strength but **did not improve bouldering performance**. **Verdict: at advanced levels, strength alone is necessary but not sufficient. The engine's multi-axis approach is validated.**
- **López-Rivera multiple** — 4-week cycles sufficient for measurable gains in 7c+/8a climbers (the cycle length the engine uses).
- **Andersen 2023 BFR** — BFR finger flexor exercise vs traditional in advanced climbers: no clear advantage in healthy climbers; useful for rehab (lower loads). **Engine v1 stance: BFR is rehab-only, not primary training.**

### 6. The 5-axis context

Finger strength is one of five axes in the assessment (D01). For redpoint goals at 7c+ or V8+, axis interaction matters more than raw finger strength:

- **Lattice n=901 bouldering predictors:** below V4 — data too sparse. V4-V8 finger strength is dominant. V9-V12 — PE and pulling become *relatively* more important. V13+ — multiple factors compress, no single predictor dominates.
- **Magiera 2013:** finger strength is the #1 of 7 variables explaining 77% of climbing performance variance. The remaining 6 include mental endurance, technique, finger endurance, and reaction-time accuracy.

Coach framing: finger strength gates *access* to higher grades but doesn't guarantee them. A user maxed on finger strength but with technique deficits will not climb their predicted grade. See `16_assessment_interpretation.md`.

### 7. Density hangs — the underrated protocol

Density hangs (Nelson, after Baar 2017 collagen-synthesis research) are the tendon-density protocol most users skip because they "look easy". Mechanism: 30-45 s yielding isometric at moderate load (40-75% MVC, RPE 9-9.5) creates a sliding effect in tendon fascicles that promotes collagen synthesis. Baar 2017 showed 10-minute loading saturates the collagen synthesis pathway and needs ~6 hours to reset — once-daily protocols are sufficient for the tendon adaptation pathway.

Canonical Nelson density-hang session:
- 2-3 hold positions (e.g. 20 mm half-crimp, 35 mm open-hand, sloper rung).
- 20-40 s per rep at ~75% MVC (RPE 9-9.5 — last 5-10 s should feel like a struggle).
- 2-3 reps per set to failure.
- 2-3 sets per position.
- 3-5 minutes rest between sets.
- ~8 total sets per session is the upper limit.
- Open-hand preferred for tendon-loading specificity.

Engine prescription: density hangs sit in the **Base phase** alongside ARC. They build the structural foundation (collagen density, pulley capacity) that lets max-strength protocols later in the cycle produce gains without injury. The user often asks "can I skip density and just do max hangs?" — answer: yes, and you'll plateau at the loads your tendons can absorb, which won't be where your muscles want to go. Density work raises that ceiling.

Common errors:
- **Going too short** (10-15 s holds) → trains alactic/recruitment instead of tendon density. Different stimulus, different adaptation. Stay 30+ s.
- **Going too easy** (60% MVC, RPE 7) → undercuts the stimulus. RPE 9 with last few seconds feeling like a struggle is the target.
- **Skipping after a few weeks** "because the gains aren't visible" → density gains are *capacity* (your max hang ceiling rises), not directly visible. The signal shows up when max hangs progress without plateauing on tendon discomfort.

### 8. Hangboard session placement in the week

D49 (no MaxHangs + IntHangs same mesocycle) governs *cycle*-level method selection. *Within* a week, hangboard placement matters too:

- **Max hangs / recruitment work**: before climbing, fresh tendons, max neural quality. Hörst rule. Hangboard should never follow heavy climbing within the same session.
- **Repeaters / IntHangs**: can follow climbing (less recruitment-dependent, more metabolic). Won't be optimal but won't be wasted.
- **Density hangs**: can stand alone or follow easy climbing. The duration-under-tension stimulus is robust to mild fatigue.
- **Overcoming isometrics (Nelson recruitment)**: same as max hangs — fresh, before climbing.
- **48 hours minimum** between two heavy hangboard sessions in the same week. CNS and tendon recovery both need this window.
- **No max hangs the day before limit bouldering**. Same neural-recovery pattern; both compete for the same recovery system.

The engine handles scheduling automatically. When the user asks why hangboard is on Monday and not Wednesday, the answer is usually "because Wednesday has limit bouldering planned and the 48-hour neural-recovery window matters more than the calendar".

### 9. Plateau diagnosis — finger strength specifically

When the user reports "my hangboard load isn't progressing", the differential matters:

| Pattern | Likely cause | Coach action |
|---|---|---|
| Load plateaued 3+ sessions, RPE constant, no pain | Cycle complete — adaptation hit ceiling. Need cycle break or method change. | Cite D49 — alternate method next cycle; the same protocol forever produces diminishing returns. |
| Load plateaued, RPE rising session-over-session | Cumulative fatigue. Recovery, not effort, is the bottleneck. | Cite D14 — defer 1-2 days, ARC + light traversing, retry. If pattern continues, deload week. |
| Load plateaued, finger or pulley discomfort | Tendon capacity reaching limit. **Stop the protocol.** | Refer to `10_injuries_fingers.md`. Modify to density hangs (lower load, tendon-friendly) until discomfort resolves. |
| Load plateaued, climbing grade also stuck | Multi-axis bottleneck, not finger-only. | Run the 5-axis review (`16_assessment_interpretation.md`) — finger may not be the limit. |
| Load plateaued, beginner (<2 yr systematic) | Normal pace — climbing volume + tendon adaptation are doing most of the work. | Don't push the protocol. The plateau is signal that the protocol has done its current job; let climbing carry the load. |

### 10. Recovery between finger sessions

Finger flexor recovery has two timescales:
- **Muscular**: 24-48 hours, fast.
- **Tendon / pulley**: 48-72+ hours, slower; the rate-limiting factor for max protocols.

The 48-hour minimum between heavy hangboard sessions is set by tendon, not muscle. Climbers who try 3+ max-hangboard sessions per week typically develop A2 pulley irritation or pulley thickening within 6-12 weeks — the muscle wants more frequency than the tendon can absorb.

Between sessions, the supporting actions that matter:
- **Sleep ≥7 hours** (Watson 2017 — sub-7-hour sleep ~2× injury risk).
- **Protein 0.4 g/kg post-training** + sufficient daily protein 1.6-2.2 g/kg.
- **Collagen 15 g + vitamin C 50 mg 30-60 min pre-finger-training** (Shaw 2017; D67).
- **Active recovery** (easy traversing, mobility) rather than total inactivity — `09_recovery_sleep.md`.

The user asking "how often can I hangboard?" should hear: 2 sessions/week is the standard sustainable rate. Three sessions/week is feasible for some advanced users with excellent recovery practices but pushes the tendon ceiling — every climber who works at the 3×/week rate accepts higher injury exposure.

## How the engine applies this

- **Catalog:** the engine carries multiple hangboard exercises spanning the López and Nelson protocol catalog (max hangs at varying durations, repeaters 7/3, density hangs, warm-up repeaters on 40 mm edge, overcoming isometrics).
- **Phase-aware selection:** Base phase prefers density hangs + repeaters (tendon + capacity); Strength & Power phase prefers max hangs + overcoming isometrics; PE phase carries maintenance hangboard (max 1×/wk).
- **D49 enforcement:** the engine prevents combining MaxHangs and IntHangs within the same mesocycle. If a user requests both, the coach explains adaptation specificity and the planner picks one.
- **D35 gate:** users with <2 years systematic training cannot select max hangs, weighted hangs, or Anderson Repeaters from the catalog (L0 hard gate).
- **D72 enforcement:** full crimp grip is never assigned to hangboard exercises. Wall-climbing prescriptions are grip-agnostic.
- **D85 test:** finger strength axis tested via `test_max_hang_7s` (and 5s alias) on 20 mm edge, half crimp.
- **Load monitoring (D14):** if edge depth or weight drops materially session-over-session, closed-loop layer triggers a recovery prescription.

## When user asks…

**"Should I do MaxHangs or IntHangs this cycle?"**

Look at the current phase first. Base/PE phase → IntHangs (repeaters) for capacity and tendon work. Strength & Power phase → MaxHangs for neural recruitment. Don't do both in one cycle (D49) — adaptation signals get mixed. Across two consecutive cycles, alternation is fine and supported (López-Rivera 2018).

**"I've been reading about Abrahangs — should I add them?"**

Cite Gilmore 2024 honestly: suggestive, retrospective evidence; additive +5.8% when combined with Max Hangs in a population with the protocol overhead. Trade-off: 2×/day for the cycle duration. Opt-in for users with ≥6 months systematic hangboard. If they're earlier than that, defer — climbing volume + standard hangboard work is enough.

**"Lattice now recommends lifting edge over hangboard — should I switch?"**

Both are valid; no head-to-head RCT exists yet. Lifting edge advantages: portable, finer load gradation, lower joint stress. Hangboard advantages: standardized testing setup, established max protocols, broad protocol catalog. If the user has a hangboard and is making progress: no reason to switch. If they're traveling, returning from injury, or want supplementary low-load frequency: lifting edge is good.

**"My max hang is BW + 25 kg on 20 mm. Is that good?"**

See `16_assessment_interpretation.md` for percentiles. Short version: for an advanced male (~75 kg BW), 25 kg added at 7 s on 20 mm is around the V7/7c+ range per Lattice norms. Frame as one axis of five — pull-up strength, PE, and technique together determine the climbing grade more than finger strength alone (Lattice n=901 data).

**"Can I do MaxHangs and IntHangs in the same week?"**

No (D49). Mixing methods within a mesocycle muddles the adaptation signal — neural recruitment work (MaxHangs) needs a different recovery context than capacity work (IntHangs). Alternate across cycles instead: this cycle MaxHangs, next cycle IntHangs. Cite López-Rivera 2018 — the combination program in that study showed no synergy.

**"My fingers are tight at warm-up — should I skip the session?"**

Yes, if tightness is persistent or sharp. The engine has a load-monitoring rule (D14): if hangboard performance drops >25% in weight or >2 mm in edge from the prior session, treat as excessive fatigue and defer 1-2 days. Tightness at warm-up is a downstream indicator. Skip the session, add an active recovery day (easy traversing, G-Tox shakes per `09_recovery_sleep.md`), reassess next training day.

**"I'm a beginner — why isn't there hangboard in my plan?"**

Cite D35 + Nelson framing: tendon adaptation lags muscular adaptation by 6-18 months. Below ~2 years of systematic training, climbing volume itself is your finger training — it builds the supporting tissues at the right pace. Hangboard before then buys risk without proportional gain. The engine reintroduces hangboard with warm-up repeaters at 35-40 mm (Nelson beginner pattern) once the gate opens.
